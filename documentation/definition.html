How to complete this template

The product definition (in short, among the branch documents, the definition) decides what the product offers and how its offer reaches the consumer, before any table exists. It is the first of the branch documents: definition, profile, design, test plan, implementation guide, post-implementation guide. The others take their questions, metrics and releases from it (Ray, 2026-10-27). Work in order: the product and its consumer; the questions in the consumer’s words; the metric qualifier rows; the open points the product owners settle; the named releases; the grains and tables, one at a time. The template is elaborated through use: a section that does not fit a product says so with its reason, and the gap is fed back to knowledge 36.

1. The product

The consumer is Mary Mink, Director of Data Architecture and Governance at the distributor, asking from her own Claude through the DV Digest connector, which serves governed metrics only: a metric and its qualifiers. The decision the product serves: understanding what the distributor bought when it acquired Sunbelt Senior, how its business reaches the carriers, and how far the arriving data can be trusted.

Capabilities offered, by named release (section 5): the agency’s business now; the history and origin of every record next; people across client numbers and agencies after that. Not offered yet: premium, which no source sends; and anything needing a second acquired agency, which the course’s source data does not yet include.

Name the consumer, the decision the product serves, and the capabilities the product owners judge to have business value. State what the product deliberately does not offer yet.

9. Compliance: questions we propose

Added 2026-10-28 (Ray, answer 1y), from Claude’s research of 2026-10-27, at Ray’s direction: data architecture is scaffolding in the business’s eyes, while compliance is the heart of a director of data governance’s function, which exists to keep the company out of fines and lawsuits. These questions are ours, not Mary’s (numbered C-1 to C-8 since 2026-10-28, renamed from P-1 to P-8 so they never collide with a profile’s results, which the profiling standard numbers P-n): proposed to her, PROPOSED until she or Ray agrees each, and kept or dropped by what her use of them shows (the product stance, Pilot ). Every finding a detector writes cites the rule it breaks: the regulation, its section, the state’s version and its citation, kept as governed data with effective dates (Ray, 2026-10-27).

The pattern the research shows: the costliest failures are rarely dramatic fraud; they are ordinary data nobody connected: a consent no one could prove, a death the company knew of but did not act on, a replacement nobody counted.

# Proposed question Why it matters (real cases) What detects it Data still needed Release
C-1 Which calls to prospects had no provable consent, or were made before it? Telephone Consumer Protection Act suits are the industry’s busiest lawsuit engine: 507 class actions in the first quarter of 2025, up 112% on a year earlier, nearly 80% of TCPA suits filed as class actions. A court certified a class of up to $30 million against Liberty Mutual over leads bought from an aggregator; the FTC settled with Assurance IQ for $100 million and MediaAlpha for $45 million over deceptive lead generation Each call joined to its consent record (lead seller, time, form); calls without one or before it Lead, consent and call records Release 2 (as of the call’s date)
C-2 Which Medicare appointments lacked a documented scope of appointment, or came too soon after it? CMS requires the beneficiary’s documented agreement to the scope of an appointment before it, and a 48-hour gap between the agreement and the meeting; the 2027 proposed rule would remove the 48 hours, so the rule in force on each date decides Appointments against their scope agreements and the hours between, by the rule in force that day Medicare appointment and scope-of-appointment records Release 2
C-3 Which seniors’ policies were replaced soon after purchase, by which agents, with or without the replacement disclosure? Rhode Island’s April 2026 consent agreement with Aflac covers replacements from 2022 to 2024 completed without the required internal replacement disclosure; California revoked an agent who repeatedly had seniors surrender annuities for new ones whose surrender fees ate their principal; Florida made twisting an annuity to a senior a third-degree felony Replacements within a short window for the same client, by agent, client aged 65 and over, disclosure present or not Policy history with replacements, client birth dates, disclosures Release 2 (history), release 3 (the same client across agencies)
C-4 Which premiums are paid from one account for unrelated clients, or by the agent, and which policies lapse right after the commission? Two California agents submitted more than 600 fraudulent life applications, nearly $2 million in commissions, with premiums paid from the conspirators’ own accounts; a Texas agent was sentenced to 292 months on schemes paying 95 to 105 percent of first-year premium Shared payment accounts, phones or addresses across unrelated clients; agent-paid premiums (rebating); early lapses after commission Premium payment records, commission records, contact details Release 3 (the same person, account or phone across records)
C-5 Which clients appear on a deceased list while their policies stay in force with no claim? Insurers used the Social Security Death Master File to stop annuity payments but not to find life policyholders who had died; MetLife paid nearly $500 million, and regulators reached multistate settlements with 27 life insurers Clients matched to a deceased list against in-force policies without a claim; the same fact used for the company but not for the customer A deceased list (invented, in the shape of the Death Master File) Release 3 (matching people)
C-6 Which sales were made by an agent not licensed, appointed or trained for that line, state and date? NAIC Producer Licensing Model Act (a license required to sell, solicit or negotiate; appointment filed within 15 days, with states differing on sooner or later); NAIC Model #275 (a one-time four-hour annuity course, verified by the insurer before solicitation) Each sale against the agent’s license, appointment and training as of the sale date A licensing feed: licenses, lines, states, dates, appointments, training Release 2
C-7 Which permanent life or annuity sales show money-laundering signs, and which agents sold them without anti-money-laundering training? FinCEN’s rule for insurers (31 CFR Part 1025) covers permanent life and annuities, requires a written program integrating agents and brokers, their training, and suspicious activity reports Third-party payers, early surrender of large single premiums, sales by untrained agents Payment and surrender records, training records Release 3
C-8 Which models and outside data sources touch a consumer decision, and have they been tested for unfair discrimination? Colorado’s Regulation 10-1-1 (implementing SB21-169) requires life insurers using external consumer data to keep board oversight, an inventory, testing, monitoring and annual reporting, and does not carve out agents An inventory of models and the outside data each uses: a data governance catalogue by another name The model and data inventory Outside the three releases; a governance catalogue

What the releases turn out to be: release 2’s history (what was true on a date, by the rule in force on that date) serves C-1, C-2, C-3 and C-6; release 3’s people-matching serves C-3, C-4, C-5 and C-7. All three releases are compliance capabilities as much as analytics.
