0. Target ERD

The vault this design builds, drawn from the source-to-target map (section 6.1) and from the profile’s source ERD: four hubs, three links and four satellites, in plain names with each table’s type in parentheses, and Pinecrest Mutual’s policy details, Lab 1-2’s extension (D-37 to D-39), dashed. Each link row and each satellite row belongs to exactly one hub row or link row; a hub row has zero or more of each, and the zero is real: the client hub holds A-1033, which arrived only in the sales data and has no client details. The dated views (the _s1 views) are left out.

Every line is drawn solid because the hash keys are computed from the same business keys on both sides: read on 2026-10-26, none of the three links’ 45 rows and none of the four satellites’ rows lacks its parent. Every line is checked by a relationships test, the links’ lines by D-42’s six and the satellites’ by D-40’s four; the layer 4 assertion assert_increment_2_link_hub_keys_exist checks the links’ lines a second way, and D-43 declares every line as a foreign key in the warehouse.

1. Purpose

Build the first piece of a classic Data Vault for the DIGEST Data Vault Course: Sunbelt Senior’s client file lands in the LANDING database, a staging model prepares it, and a client hub records every client number Sunbelt Senior has sent, with the time it first arrived and where it came from.

The increment teaches arrival and identity: what a source table, a load timestamp and a record source are; what staging may and may not change; how a business key differs from a technical key; how a business key becomes a hash key; and what a hub and its ghost records hold. The data is synthetic and specified by the agreed profile, whose eight scenarios are the eight behavioral paths of the staging model and the hub.

Increment 1.1 gives every vault row a permanent record source code instead of a name, and keeps each code’s name, description and owner in one source system seed. A rename then changes one row of the seed, recorded in git, and no vault row. A three-row table the team edits needs no vault objects to keep its history.

Increment 2 builds the first two-source raw vault. Sunbelt Senior’s policy sales data and Northfield Life’s policy data land beside Sunbelt Senior’s client list, and the vault records each source’s roles at its own grain, the policies, each role’s relationship to a policy, and the agents who sold and wrote the policies, with descriptive history. The two sources meet at the policy, matched on the carrier’s number, while one person stays under two unrelated numbers, one per source: A-1007 at Sunbelt Senior and NLI-55007 at Northfield Life.

3. Concept

A Data Vault requires every row to carry its record source and load date, so values can be traced back to the source (Wikipedia, Data vault modeling, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_vault_modeling). Linstedt and Olschimke define hard business rules as never changing the meaning of incoming data, only the way it is stored, and list the consolidation of data from multiple sources among the soft business rules (Building a Scalable Data Warehouse with Data Vault 2.0, section 2.2, read from Ray’s copy on 2026-10-22). So the vault does not consolidate data from different sources; sources meet in one place only: a hub holds one row per business key, shared by every source that carries it (Snowflake, Data Vault techniques: hub tables are always multi-source and unique by business object, https://snowflake.com/blog/data-vault-techinques-hub-locking). Staging applies only hard business rules, such as type casting, formatting and null handling; rules that change data according to business requirements are soft business rules, applied in the business vault (VaultSpeed, Data Vault 2.0 concepts, https://docs.vaultspeed.com/space/VPD/3011870744.md; GFT, Data Vault 2.0, https://www.gft.com/pl/en/blog/data-vault-2-0-jako-nowa-metodyka-projektowania-i-implementowania-hurtowni-danych). All read 2026-10-23. Increment 1 builds the vault’s first two pieces; increment 2 adds the policy, a second role, and the relationships and descriptions.

Staging takes one landed source table and prepares it without changing its meaning. It enforces types (the load timestamp arrives as text and becomes a timestamp), fixes the record source, and computes the hash key. These are hard rules: they change how a value is stored, never what it means.

The hub is the list of business keys of one concept, here Sunbelt Senior’s client numbers. Each row holds the hash key, the business key, the load timestamp of the key’s first arrival and the record source of that arrival. Later sightings of a key add nothing. Two ghost records, one for an unknown key and one for an error, complete later equi-joins: a point-in-time table references a satellite’s ghost record until a real record exists (Snowflake, point-in-time constructs and join trees, https://www.snowflake.com/en/blog/point-in-time-constructs-and-join-trees, read 2026-10-23); the toolkit creates them in staging, with the record sources SYSTEM and ERROR by default (datavault4dbt’s global variables default_unknown_rsrc and default_error_rsrc).

The hub records identifiers, not resolved people. If one person reached Sunbelt Senior under two client numbers, the hub holds two rows and cannot tell; that is identity work for a later increment.

Roles at their sources’ grain. A source’s number identifies what the source keeps one row for. Sunbelt Senior’s client list keeps one row per client, so A-1007 identifies a client: a role, not a person. Northfield Life’s NLI-55007 identifies an insured. Each role gets its own hub, keyed by its own number; the person behind the roles is in no source, and matching it belongs after the raw vault.

Links and satellites. A link records a relationship between hub keys at the grain its source sends it: the sales data relates a client to a policy in a role, and the carrier data relates an insured to a policy. A satellite holds one source’s descriptive columns for one hub or link, with a new version whenever they change.

A key an outside issuer gave. The vault matches sources only where they carry the same key. A client number and an insured number are each issued by one company, so they never match. An agent’s national producer number is assigned through the National Insurance Producer Registry and is the same in every source, so the agent hub matches an agent sold through both sources without any rule.

6.3 Domain content

The hash rule, step by step (datavault4dbt code, read 2026-10-22): the client number is cast to text, trimmed, and checked for NULL; text is escaped and quoted; the whole input is uppercased because business keys are case-insensitive by default (D-8); line breaks and tabs are removed; the result is hashed with MD5. Only a NULL input becomes the all-zeros key. D-4 moves empty and whitespace-only values into that NULL path.

Ghost records. The staging view adds an unknown row (all-zeros key, business key (unknown), load timestamp 0001-01-01 00:00:01, record source SYSTEM) and an error row (all-f key, business key (error), load timestamp 8888-12-31 23:59:59, record source ERROR). The hub keeps them like any other key, so after the build it holds 34 client keys and these two rows. No satellite stores a source row on the unknown key: each satellite reads a staging view that keeps only rows with their key (D-44), and an inspector raises every row without its key as a finding (D-45).

Keys declared from tests. Each relationships test in layer 1 names a column and the table it must match; each unique test names a table’s key. The macro declare_vault_keys reads those tests after every build and declares each as a key in Snowflake: a unique test becomes the table’s primary key, a relationships test a foreign key (D-43). Snowflake does not enforce them; they describe the vault to anything that reads its DDL, such as a modeling tool.

Keep the first sighting. For each hash key the hub keeps the earliest row across everything it reads, and on later builds inserts only keys it does not already hold. That is why a client present in both loads keeps load 1’s timestamp.

Keys with two parts. The policy key is the carrier’s code and the policy number, hashed together. Each part is standardized like a single key; a missing part is written as a placeholder (^^) before hashing, so a sale with no carrier code still gets a real key, separate from the same number under NFL. Only a key missing every part becomes the all-zeros key (datavault4dbt hash and hash_standardization macros, read at the fork’s pinned commit).

The role as part of a link’s key. client_policy_l hashes the client key, the policy key and the role together, so A-1010 as owner and A-1010 as insured on NF-100010 are two link rows. A role code the source never documented, such as payor, becomes one more row with that role; the link’s columns do not change.

6.5 Worked examples

Invented names, separate from the specified rows. Sunbelt Senior sends Rosa Diaz as A-2001 on Monday. Its Tuesday file has her again as a-2001 because a clerk typed lower case: same hash key, so the hub still shows Monday as her first arrival. Wednesday’s file carries A2001 without the hyphen: a different key, so the hub now holds two rows that both belong to Rosa Diaz, and only the form inspector says anything about it. Thursday’s file has a new client whose number field is blank: staging turns the blank into NULL, the row takes the all-zeros key, the hub gains nothing, the client details satellite stores nothing, and an inspector writes one finding naming the row.

A second story, also invented. Tomas buys a policy from Lakeview Life through an agency; he owns it, and his mother Lucia is the insured. The agency’s sales data sends two rows for policy LV-200001: Tomas as owner and Lucia as insured, each under the agency’s own client number. Lakeview Life’s data sends one row: the policy, with Lucia under Lakeview’s own insured number. The vault holds one policy, two of the agency’s clients and one Lakeview insured. Lucia appears twice, once per source, and nothing in the vault says the two are one person. The agent who sold the policy, Maria, is different: both sources send her national producer number, so the vault holds her once.
