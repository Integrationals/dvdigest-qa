Dev, QA, production

How a release reaches you

Dev

Every change is built and tested in a developer's own database first, on both dbt engines.

QA

One pull request into qa; a fresh QA build from cleared databases, as QA's own identity, certified on six gates.

Main

The certified qa squash-merged into main; qa then converges with main.

Production

Production built from main by its own job and identity, and checked against QA row for row.

Release

Tagged on the commit production built, named for the idea that holds it together.

What protects it

Guards on every build

137 tests. Data tests and unit tests across two dbt engines, every build.

Two identities. QA's builder cannot touch production, and production's cannot touch QA.

Inspectors and auditors. Records the vault cannot accept, and counts that do not reconcile, are written down, never dropped.

Baselines. Every build compares its results with the state just before it.

The standards conversation

Aligning with your standards

Data Vault practitioners follow different approaches. The principles are shared, but teams make different choices about business keys, how satellites are split, where integration happens, how releases are tested and promoted. None of these is the one right answer; each reflects a team’s experience.

This vault is one way to apply them: an approach that generally follows the shared principles, using an insurance distributor as its subject area. This site presents it, with the releases, the documentation, the model and the code, as a starting point for a conversation about standards. Yours may well differ, and where it does, we want to follow yours.

Wherever your approach would enhance it, start from the vault’s structure. Each release includes its DDL in the repository, at infra/snowflake/ddl/vault.sql and infra/snowflake/ddl/mart.sql: bring it into a modeling tool such as ER/Studio, model your standard, and send it back, by email or on a branch of your own. We build it into the vault and send you the pull request, so you can review your approach in the actual code.

Ray will send you an invitation to the repository; just tell him your GitHub username.
